## What to build

The matcher's rules stop being hand-written only. A reflector, the Assistant's model called offline, reads the words the matcher got wrong and writes generalised matching rules; the server replays each rule over the labelled events and promotes it only when it fixes the words of several accounts and breaks none; a promoted rule is a tier of the model-free matcher, so that a request still calls no model. The loop is GEPA's (Agrawal et al., "GEPA: Reflective Prompt Evolution Can Outperform Reinforcement Learning", ICLR 2026 oral, arXiv 2507.19457; [research note 11](../../research/11-self-improving-agents-2025-26.md) §3.1) with the matcher's rule table as the candidate in place of a prompt and the replay as its feedback function; the gate and the bookkeeping are RuleChef's (Kovacs, Verdha and Recski, arXiv 2607.01293, 2026; note 11 §3.2); the acceptance criteria are ASI's (Wang et al., COLM 2025; note 11 §3.3), the rule budget WALL-E 2.0's (note 11 §3.3), and the append-only rows with counters ACE's (ICLR 2026; note 11 §1.1). Tickets 03 and 06 stay as the replay's baselines: stage E is B with the induced rules, beside C and D.

**Instances.** The labelled rows of the learning curve: each `answered` Place row followed by the same account's labels of the same words (a chip, an edit, a rejection) before its next `answered` row of those words; the truth is the Place of the last chip or edit, and a row with rejections alone teaches nothing. Only accounts onboarded 7 days or more before the row (tickets 03 and 06) and only rows of the last 90 days. One instance per words and truth, with the distinct accounts behind it. A stable hash of the words holds 20 % of them out (RuleChef's development split), which the loop never sees and the gate scores. The eval command may add the gold development set's items as instances; the test set, never.

**Rules.** Two kinds, in a small language the server executes:

- A `name` rule is a pattern over a Place's normalised name and a replacement that gives a form students say it as: `^(.{1,3}?)(?:과학|과|학)?대학$` → `$1대` makes 경영대 of 경영대학, 자연대 of 자연과학대학 and 공대 of 공과대학. Its forms join the index as the generator's do, for every Place whose name the pattern fits.
- A `words` rule is a pattern over the User's words and a replacement that gives what to look up instead: `^윗(.+)$` → `상부$1`. The rewritten words are matched by the exact, generated and induced forms.

The syntax is JavaScript's RegExp with the `u` flag, restricted: literals, character classes, `.`, `\d`, anchors, alternation, groups that are at most optional, and quantifiers on single atoms, at most 80 characters; no backreference, lookaround or quantified group, so that no pattern can run away on words of at most 50 characters. A replacement is literal characters and `$1` to `$9`. A rule never names a Place: what it writes is matched by the catalog as any words are, so an induced answer is a catalog id by construction and `FoundIds` holds. Induced hits are a tier of their own, `induced`, after the exact names and the taught aliases and before the generator's forms and the loose tiers, where the rules go first as RuleChef's do: most of what the matcher gets wrong is a question or a wrong answer of the generator and the loose tiers, which a rule placed after them could never fix; the gate, the audit and the holdback are what keep a rule honest, not its place in the order. The held-back accounts of `ASSISTANT_ALIAS_HOLDBACK_PERCENT` never get the tier, as they never get everyone's aliases, so that the rules' effect can be told from habit. The source of the alias chain (ticket 06) reads with the induced rules, as they are the rules' reading today.

**The loop** (GEPA). A candidate is a rule set; the root is the active rules. For each of the run's reflector calls (12 by default): a parent is sampled from the Pareto front, the candidates right on some instance that no other candidate is right on a superset of, each weighted by how many instances it is right on (GEPA's per-instance Pareto sampling, which beat greedy and beam in its ablation); up to 6 of the parent's failures on the training instances are sampled, and the reflector is given each one's words, the expected Place with its name and the short forms the generator already makes of it, the current answer (nothing, a question with its candidates, or a wrong Place), and the parent's rules; it answers up to 3 rules. Each that compiles under the restrictions becomes a child, the parent plus the rule, kept when it is right on more of the minibatch than the parent and then scored on every training instance (GEPA's minibatch acceptance, then the full score). There is no Merge of lineages: GEPA found it hurt at times. The run is seeded, so it repeats given the reflector's answers.

**The gate** (RuleChef, ASI). The best candidate's new rules are tried one at a time on top of the active rules, and a rule is promoted only when it fixes at least 2 distinct words of at least 2 distinct accounts, the held-out words counting, since a fix there is a words the reflector never saw (a rule that fixes one words is an alias and belongs to tickets 03 and 06), fired and changed the answer on each (ASI's three criteria), and breaks no instance that was right, training or held out. Each active rule is then counted again (`fixed`, `broke`, `accounts`) and retired by leave-one-out when the set is as good without it or when it breaks an instance (RuleChef's auditor); at most 50 rules stay active, those that fix most (WALL-E 2.0's budget, whose ablation collapsed without pruning). A row is never rewritten or deleted: a retired rule keeps `retired_at`, and a rule the gate later picks again is brought back.

**When it runs.** Once a night, started by the worker server's schedule through `POST /internal/induction` with the worker token, which reaches one main server however many run; every main server re-reads the active rules each minute. By hand, `pnpm eval:induce` runs the same loop against a database and writes the rules and the run's trace to a file, never to the database, so that the replay can score rules induced before a day.

**What changes for Users.** Two accounts that write 윗공 and 아랫공, get nothing, and tap the Places they meant teach a rule that answers 윗식당 the next day, which nobody has written yet; an alias could not. The exact names and what anyone taught still answer first; a rule can fix a `none`, and a question or a wrong answer of the generator and the loose tiers. A rule that stops fixing anything retires within a night.

**Code.** `src/places/induced-rules.ts` (the language: the check, the compile, the apply), `place-index.ts` (the forms, the tier, the holdback), `place-matcher.ts` (the active rules and the minute's refresh), `src/assistant/induction/` (`instances.ts`, `scoring.ts`, `pareto.ts`, `reflector.ts`, `loop.ts`, `gate.ts`, `induction.service.ts`, `induction.controller.ts`), `ASSISTANT_INDUCTION_BUDGET` in the settings, one migration (`induced_rules`, the `induced` tier), the worker's cron, `eval/replay/induce-cli.ts` and `induced.ts`, stage E in `replay.ts`, the induced series in the learning curve. README paragraphs for the rules, the endpoint and the commands.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The language: patterns with a backreference, lookaround, a quantified group, a nested quantifier or over 80 characters are refused; replacements other than literals and `$1`–`$9` are refused; a `name` rule's form must be 2 characters or more, shorter than the name and unlike it; a `words` rule must change the words.
- [ ] The tier: an induced form answers after the exact and alias tiers and before the generated tier; a rewritten words is matched by the exact, generated and induced forms; a held-back account never gets the tier.
- [ ] Instances: labelled rows as the learning curve's, accounts 7 days old or more, the last 90 days, one per words and truth with its accounts, 20 % held out by a stable hash.
- [ ] The loop on hand-made instances with a scripted reflector: Pareto sampling keeps a candidate right on an instance no other is right on and drops a dominated one; a child is kept only when it beats its parent on the minibatch; the run repeats under one seed.
- [ ] The gate: a rule fixing one words, or two words of one account, is refused; a rule breaking a right instance, training or held out, is refused; a fix of a held-out words counts; leave-one-out retires a rule the set is as good without; the budget keeps the rules that fix most.
- [ ] Through HTTP with the scripted model as the reflector: labelled rows of two accounts for two 뷁-words whose Places their rewrite names make a `words` rule; after `POST /internal/induction` with the worker token, a third account's words are answered `one` on the `induced` tier; a held-back account is not; a request without the token is refused.
- [ ] Replay: `pnpm eval:induce` writes a rules file with its trace; `pnpm eval:replay --rules <file>` reports stage E, equal to B without a file; the learning curve reports the induced series from the files given, each point using the newest file induced before it.
- [ ] README paragraphs; `pnpm lint`, `pnpm typecheck` and the touched tests pass.

Decisions where the papers left room, each with its reason:

- **The tier sits after the exact names and the aliases and before the generator's forms**, not after them as first written. The first live run showed why: the model proposed sound rules (ㅎ관 → 학관, 학곤 → 학관, 수의과대학 → 수의대) and every one was "no gain", because most of what the matcher gets wrong is a question or a wrong answer of the generator and the loose tiers, which a later tier cannot change. RuleChef puts its rules first too; the gate, the audit and the holdback keep a rule honest, not its place in the order. The exact names and what anyone taught still win.
- **The held-out words count toward the two words a rule must fix**, and a rule may break none of them: a fix of a word the reflector never saw is the evidence of generalisation the gate asks for, and with a few dozen instances a rule that fits a family rarely finds two of its members in the training fifth. RuleChef's gate reads the held-out split the same way (a patch is kept when the held-out score does not drop). The cost: the score on the held-out words is a validation score, not a test one; the test set stays the only test, and the rules file records the training words only, so the replay never scores a word the reflector was shown.
- **A rule never names a Place.** The language writes forms and rewrites, which the catalog then matches, so `FoundIds` and every id check hold without a new path. The price is that a rule cannot settle a `several` of exact names, which is right: those are the catalog's own ambiguities.
- **The reflector is told what was tried.** Without it, a call repeats the rules the earlier calls dropped; with it, the budget buys new proposals. The prompt also carries the generator's forms of the Place meant, so the model does not propose what the generator already makes.
- **Each gold development item is its own account** when the eval adds them as instances, since the team wrote them; events' instances carry their real accounts. The server's nightly run uses events alone.
- **Reasoning tokens.** Haiku-class models spend most of their output on reasoning; a budget of 1,200 output tokens left the answer empty and silent. The reflector asks for 6,000 and the loop records the finish reason and a note when an answer held no rules, so that an empty call is visible in the trace.
- **No Merge of lineages** (GEPA's crossover), which hurt in the paper at times; **no per-rule synthetic test cases** (TellOnce), since the real labelled rows are the test; **no rule-table version on events**, left for later.

What the harness reports is the proof, not the prose: the trace of every run lists each call's minibatch and every proposal with why it was kept, dropped or refused.

### Decided (2026-11-01)

- **The induced tier stays before the generator's forms.** Most of what the matcher gets wrong is the generator's `several`, which a rule behind it could never fix; the gate, the audit and the holdback are the safety, and the one line that moves the tier is known should a rule ever beat a right hand-written form.
- **Held-out fixes keep counting toward a rule's two words** while the labelled words are few: it is what lets a family rule pass on today's data, at the price of stage E on the held-out words being a validation score, which the reports say. The gate returns to training-only fixes once a window holds some 200 labelled words; a line in `gate.ts`, when the rows are there.
- **The reflector's model is a setting.** `ASSISTANT_REFLECTOR_MODEL` (empty by default, the Assistant's model then) names the OpenRouter model the induction asks for rules, so that a stronger reflector, which proposes fewer literal rules, can be tried without touching the Assistant's own model; the gate stays what stops a weak one from polluting the rules, as ACE found weak reflectors do.
- **Nightly at 04:00 Asia/Seoul stands**: one run, 12 calls, about 20,000 tokens; more often adds nothing until the rows do.
- **The held-out fifth and the 90-day window stand** (`HOLDOUT_PERCENT`, `INSTANCE_WINDOW_DAYS`), as ticket 06's numbers do, until the final slice says otherwise.
- **No capture group is required of a rule.** `^ㅎ([ㄱ관])$ → 학생회관` is literal-looking and right: what makes a rule general is the gate's two words from two accounts, not its syntax, and a required group would only make the reflector write one for show.
